# Teesside Green Hydrogen Production: MCMC & Financial Valuation Model

This repository contains a quantitative financial model and Bayesian **MCMC** simulation analysing the public sector cost, private returns, and financial viability of a **9.5 GW Green Hydrogen Deployment (20 × 475 MW electrolysers)** in Teesside, UK.

The evaluation models the **Hydrogen Production Business Model (HPBM)** operating via a **Two-Way Contract for Difference (CfD)** under the **Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreement (LCHA)** over a 15-year support window.

**Files:** `hydrogen_monte_carlo.py` (runnable model) · `hydrogen_monte_carlo.ipynb` (same model, cell-by-cell) · `mcmc_diagnostics.png` (output figure regenerated by the script)

##  MECHANICS: HYDROGEN PRODUCTION BUSINESS MODEL (HPBM)

The project uses a **Two-Way Contract for Difference (CfD)** between the UK Government and private facility operators under the **Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreement (LCHA)**:

1. **Risk Allocation:**
   * **Private Sector:** bears 100% of construction, capital expenditure, and operational risk.
   * **Government:** bears 100% of the market/off-take price volatility risk.
2. **Pricing Structure:**
   * **Agreed Strike Price:** £241.00/MWh (covers production cost + agreed private return margin; HAR1 weighted average).
   * **End-User Market Price:** £52.00/MWh central case (grey/fossil hydrogen market parity).
   * **Clawback Provision:** if the market price exceeds the Strike Price during the 15-year period, excess profits are returned to the UK Treasury. The model represents this with a **two-regime market price process**: a parity regime (noisy draw around £52) and a crisis regime (15%-probability year, lognormal multiplier with median 3× and a hard cap at 6× parity, reflecting the observed 2022 TTF gas spike of ~4–6×). The cap prevents the unbounded lognormal tail from overstating clawback risk — an earlier version of this model let the tail run to ~28× parity, which was economically implausible.
3. **Infrastructure Enabling:**
   * Teesside development framework offers pre-approved industrial land, pipeline connections, and expedited planning.

##  METHODOLOGY: FROM MONTE CARLO TO MCMC

A plain Monte Carlo model draws every uncertain input independently from a triangular distribution and pushes the draws through the cashflow equations once. That treats each input as a fixed guess and never asks which parameter combinations are actually consistent with observed market data.

This model is **Bayesian**:

1. **State variables.** The inferred parameter vector is

   $$\theta = (\text{strike},\ \text{gilt},\ \text{CPI},\ \text{parity})$$

   plus two simulated state processes in Stage 2: the annual market price $m_t$ (two-regime) and the annual capacity factor (triangular 45/50/60%). Everything else is a fixed, sourced input (table above).

2. **Priors.** Each parameter gets a triangular prior (min / mode / max), chosen to be wide enough that the data, not the prior, moves the posterior where it can:

   | Parameter | Prior (min / mode / max) | Justification |
   |---|---|---|
   | Strike price (£/MWh) | 220 / 241 / 260 | HAR1 weighted average £241; contract range across the 10 HAR1 projects |
   | Gilt rate (%) | 4.50 / 5.34 / 6.50 | Sep 2026 10-yr gilt ~5.3–5.4%; band covers recent gilt-market volatility |
   | CPI inflation (%) | 1.50 / 2.00 / 3.50 | BoE target 2%; band covers 2021–2024 inflation experience |
   | Grey-H₂ parity (£/MWh) | 35 / 52 / 75 | SMR cost floor to upper industrial gas-price band |

3. **Likelihood.** One observation is used: a grey-hydrogen parity benchmark of £52/MWh with £8 observation noise (Gaussian likelihood). This updates **only** the parity parameter. Strike, gilt, and CPI are policy/macro quantities set outside the market observation, so they stay prior-driven — stating this explicitly is part of honest Bayesian modelling.

4. **Posterior via MCMC.** A **Metropolis–Hastings** sampler (written from scratch, ~30 lines, symmetric Gaussian proposal) explores the joint posterior:

   $$P(\theta \mid y) \propto P(y \mid \theta)\, P(\theta)$$

   Configuration: 4 chains × 25,000 retained draws (5,000 burn-in, thinning = 5), tuned so acceptance ≈ 0.375 (the classic optimal range for random-walk MH).

5. **Convergence diagnostics.** The sampler is only trusted if:
   * **R-hat (Gelman–Rubin)** < 1.01 across all 4 chains — achieved: max 1.0001.
   * **ESS** (effective sample size, Geyer initial-positive-pair truncation of the autocorrelation time) is large — achieved: 20,488–49,957 per parameter.

6. **Posterior predictive.** Converged posterior draws feed the 15-year CfD cashflow. For each posterior draw $d$ and year $t$:

   * Subsidy rate: $s_d = \text{strike}_d \cdot g_d / (1+g_d)$ (the derivation below, applied per draw)
   * Gross outflow: $\text{outflow}_{d,t} = V_d \cdot s_d \cdot (1+\pi_d)^{t-1}$, discounted at $(1+g_d)^t$
   * Market price: $m_{d,t} = \text{parity}_d \cdot \epsilon_{d,t}$ normally, or $\times\,\text{lognormal}(3, 0.5)$ capped at 6× in a crisis year
   * Clawback: $\text{clawback}_{d,t} = \max(m_{d,t} - \text{strike}_d(1+\pi_d)^{t-1},\,0) \cdot V_d$
   * Net government NPV = gross NPV − clawback PV

##  FINANCIAL MODELING & SUBSIDY CALCULATIONS (deterministic base case)

All quantities at prior modes: strike £241, gilt 5.34%, CPI 2.0%, CF 50%.

### 1. Annual energy volume

$$\text{Annual Volume} = 9.5\,\text{GW} \times 8{,}760\,\text{h} \times 50\% = 41{,}610{,}000\ \text{MWh} = 41.61\ \text{TWh}$$

$$\text{Capped Volume} = 41{,}610{,}000 \times 75\% = 31{,}207{,}500\ \text{MWh}$$

### 2. Base subsidy rate (£/MWh)

$$\text{Gilt-adjusted market price} = \frac{\text{Strike}}{1+\text{Gilt}} = \frac{£241.00}{1.0534} = £228.78/\text{MWh}$$

$$\text{Subsidy rate} = £241.00 - £228.78 = £12.2170/\text{MWh}$$

### 3. Year-1 gross government outflow

$$\text{Year-1 Outflow} = 31{,}207{,}500\ \text{MWh} \times £12.2170 = \mathbf{£381{,}262{,}388.93}$$

### 4. 15-year nominal support outflow (2.0% CPI compounding)

$$\sum_{t=1}^{15} £381{,}262{,}388.93 \times 1.02^{t-1} = \mathbf{£6{,}593{,}329{,}446.25}$$

### 5. Government NPV (gross of clawback)

$$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{15} \frac{£381{,}262{,}388.93 \times 1.02^{t-1}}{1.0534^{t}} = \mathbf{£4{,}374{,}916{,}685.37}$$

### 6. CapEx

$$\text{CapEx} = 9{,}500{,}000\ \text{kW} \times £1{,}500/\text{kW} = \mathbf{£14.25\ Billion}$$

(This replaces the earlier £2.80B / £140M-per-unit figure, which implied £295/kW — well below any Western installed-cost benchmark. £1,500/kW sits at the plausible midpoint between Chinese equipment prices and Western turnkey installed costs.)

### 7. Carbon offset revenue (displacement basis)

Carbon revenue is credited **only on the 5 TWh/yr industrial offtake that displaces existing unabated SMR hydrogen** on Teesside — not on total production. Crediting the full 832 kt output would double-count hydrogen that simply supplies new demand.

$$\text{Displaced H}_2 = \frac{5 \times 10^9\ \text{kWh}}{50\ \text{kWh/kg}} = 100{,}000{,}000\ \text{kg} = 100\ \text{kt H}_2/\text{yr}$$

$$\text{Avoided CO}_2 = 100{,}000{,}000\ \text{kg} \times 10\ \text{kg CO}_2/\text{kg H}_2 = 1{,}000{,}000\ \text{t CO}_2/\text{yr}$$

$$\text{Carbon revenue} = 1{,}000{,}000 \times £49.41 = \mathbf{£49.41\ Million/yr}$$

(The earlier £74.12M figure was based on 150 kt of H₂ from 5 TWh at 33.3 kWh/kg — but the model's own electrolyser intensity is 50 kWh/kg, and it credited displacement on volumes that were never supplying industrial demand. Both errors are corrected here.)

##  STATE VARIABLES & MODEL ASSUMPTIONS

**Inferred state (Stage 1, MCMC):**

| Symbol | Variable | Prior | Updated by data? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\theta_1$ | Strike price | Tri(220, 241, 260) £/MWh | No — policy parameter |
| $\theta_2$ | Gilt rate | Tri(4.5, 5.34, 6.5) % | No — macro parameter |
| $\theta_3$ | CPI inflation | Tri(1.5, 2.0, 3.5) % | No — macro parameter |
| $\theta_4$ | Grey-H₂ parity | Tri(35, 52, 75) £/MWh | **Yes** — Gaussian likelihood on £52 ± £8 |

**Simulated state (Stage 2, posterior predictive):**

| Symbol | Process | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| $m_t$ | Market price, year $t$ | Parity regime: $\mathcal{N}(\theta_4, 8^2)$; crisis regime (p=0.15): $\theta_4 \times \text{Lognormal}(\ln 3, 0.5)$ capped at 6× |
| $\phi$ | Capacity factor | Tri(0.45, 0.50, 0.60), one draw per posterior sample |
| $\epsilon$ | Carbon realisation | Tri(0.85, 1.0, 1.15) |

**Fixed assumptions (sourced in the table above):** 20 × 475 MW fleet; £1,500/kW installed CapEx; 50 kWh/kg electrolyser intensity; 75% LCHA volume cap; 15-year support; 10 kg CO₂/kg H₂ SMR displacement intensity; £49.41/t UK ETS price; 5 TWh/yr industrial offtake displacement basis.

**Key structural assumptions (judgement, stated openly):**
* The subsidy formula (strike discounted at the gilt rate) is a stylisation of HPBM difference payments, not the actual LCHA pricing mechanism, which references a fuel-feedstock-dependent variable price. It keeps the model simple and is applied consistently in every calculation.
* Carbon revenue is presented as a potential co-benefit, not netted against government subsidy cost.
* The 15% crisis probability is an expert-judgement prior, not a fitted frequency.

##  ECONOMIC JUSTIFICATION & PUBLIC FINANCE IMPACT

* **Budgetary Impact:** the 15-year nominal subsidy total (£6.59B) is **~0.55% of one year's UK public spending (~£1.2T)** ([OBR Public Finances](https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/brief-guides-and-explainers/public-finances/)); the average annual burden is ~0.03% of annual spend.
* **Replacing Energy Relief Subsidies:** the UK has allocated £7B+ subsidising energy-intensive industries facing high power bills ([Gov.uk Energy Relief Scheme](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-cuts-electricity-bill-for-10000-manufacturers-in-boost-for-uk-competitiveness)). Transitioning industrial manufacturing to locally produced Teesside green hydrogen provides long-term energy security without ongoing emergency relief interventions.
* **Socioeconomic Benefits:** accelerates private capital investment into the Teesside industrial cluster, boosting local highly-skilled employment, regional wage scales, and technical human capital.
